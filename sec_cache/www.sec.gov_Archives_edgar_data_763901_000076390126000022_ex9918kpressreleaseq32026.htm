CEO COMMENTARY
Jorge J. García, President and Chief Executive Officer, said:
"We are pleased with our third quarter results. Our financial performance was solid, and we continued to advance our three
strategic objectives: being the number one bank for our customers, being simple and efficient, and being a top-performing
bank."
"We reported earnings per share of $4.21, 3% lower than the second quarter and 34% higher than the third quarter of 2025,
and a ROTCE of 15.80%. These results reflected higher net interest income, stable underlying credit trends and continued
capital return, partially offset by higher operating expenses and lower non-interest income. During the quarter, we
repurchased $179 million of common stock and declared a 20% increase in our quarterly common stock dividend to $0.90
per share."
"Business activity in Puerto Rico remains healthy, supported by a strong labor market. Credit metrics continue to be stable
while we remain attentive to economic conditions, given the potential impact of prolonged inflation and higher cost of fuel
and energy."
"It is a privilege to step into this role with a strong capital position, a solid banking franchise, a talented team and meaningful
momentum. Our responsibility is to build on those strengths and continue creating long-term value for our customers,
shareholders, colleagues, and communities."
"I am grateful to our team for their commitment and the results they deliver every day."

Popular, Inc. – Net interest income ("NII") of $699 million increased $6 million from Q2 2026, driven by higher income from
investment securities and commercial loans, as well as the impact of one additional day in the quarter. The increase was

higher rates on such deposits. Average earning assets increased $677 million, primarily attributable to higher U.S. Treasury
securities balances due to re-investment of maturities at higher yields, coupled with higher average commercial loans. Total
average deposits increased by $502 million, driven by an average increase of $1.1 billion in Puerto Rico public deposits,

points to 1.13%. The additional day in the quarter contributed $6 million to net interest income in Q3 2026.
NII fully taxable equivalent ("FTE") and NIM FTE (Non-GAAP) – NII FTE of $802 million increased $13 million from Q2

Loan yields FTE increased by three basis points to 7.56%.

Banco Popular de Puerto Rico ("BPPR") Segment – NII of $592 million increased $2 million from Q2 2026. The increase
was primarily due to an increase in income from money market and investment securities by $14 million or 10 basis points
as a result of higher average balances and higher yields of U.S. Treasury securities and higher income from loans by $5
million, driven by higher average balances, mainly in the mortgage and personal loans portfolios and by one additional day
in the quarter. Higher interest expense on deposits of $17 million was primarily due to higher average balances on the
market-linked P.R. public deposits by $1.1 billion and an 11 basis points increase in the rates of those deposits. NIM
declined four basis points during Q3 2026 to 3.81%. Deposit costs increased by nine basis points to 1.41%, including the
costs of P.R. public deposits which increased 11 basis points to 2.72% for the period. Excluding P.R. public deposits, deposit
costs increased by two basis points.
Popular Bank ("PB") Segment – NII of $117 million increased $4 million, or 3%, from Q2 2026. The increase was mainly
due to higher income from loans by $6 million, mainly due to commercial loans with higher income by $8 million and higher
yields by 11 basis points, driven by new originations carrying higher yields, partially offset by higher interest expense by $1
million attributable to higher average balances on time deposits. NIM expanded three basis points to 3.20%. Deposit costs
increased by one basis point to 2.74%.

During the third quarter of 2026, the Corporation’s overall credit quality remained stable. Consumer and mortgage portfolio
performance remained favorable, with historically low net charge-offs (despite a slight seasonal increase in the auto
portfolio), historically low delinquency levels, and net recoveries in the mortgage portfolio. Changes in commercial credit
metrics during the quarter were driven by borrower-specific developments; core commercial portfolios continue to show
stable credit trends.
Non-Performing Loans ("NPLs") and Net Charge Offs ("NCOs")
Total NPLs held-in-portfolio increased $52 million to $466 million during Q3 2026. Excluding consumer loans, inflows of
NPLs held-in-portfolio decreased $56 million in the third quarter of 2026. The ratio of NPLs to total loans held in the portfolio
was 1.16% for the third quarter of 2026, compared to 1.04% for the previous quarter. NPLs held-for-sale decreased $84
million driven by the sale during Q3 of an NPL held-for-sale.
NCO Ratio of 0.73% decreased 32 basis points when compared to the previous quarter. Total NCOs decreased by $31
million quarter-over-quarter, reflecting lower commercial charge-offs.
6
BPPR segment - NPLs held-in-portfolio increased $54 million, primarily driven by a $52 million increase in commercial
NPLs. The increase includes the inflow to NPL of a $74 million commercial and industrial ("C&I") loan, partially offset by a
$34 million partial charge-off related to a commercial NPL that had entered non-performing status in the prior quarter. These
events are considered borrower-specific and are not indicative of broader deterioration in the commercial portfolio. Excluding
consumer loans, BPPR segment NPL inflows decreased $45 million compared to the prior quarter. 
NCOs decreased $30 million, primarily reflecting lower commercial charge-offs by $38 million. This variance resulted from
the prior quarter, which included the resolution of a commercial relationship that resulted in a $71 million charge-off, partially
offset by the previously mentioned commercial charge-off of $34 million in Q3 2026. NCO Ratio of 1.03% decreased 43
basis points quarter-over-quarter.
PB segment - NPLs held-in-portfolio decreased by $2 million, while inflows to NPLs, excluding consumer loans, decreased
$11 million compared to the previous quarter. NCO Ratio of 0.03%, decreased five basis points during the quarter.
Refer to table L for a breakdown of Non-Performing Assets.
Allowance for loan losses ("ACL")
The ACL as of September 30, 2026 amounted to $771 million, a decrease of $14 million when compared to the second
quarter of 2026. The decline was driven primarily by improved consumer credit performance, in part offset by higher
reserves for specific commercial exposures.
BPPR segment - The ACL decreased by $11 million compared to the previous quarter, mostly driven by a $17 million
decrease in reserves for consumer loans due to favorable changes in the credit composition of the auto and personal loans
portfolios. These decreases were partially offset by higher reserves for commercial loans, mostly associated with C&I NPL
inflows during the last two quarters, changes in macroeconomic scenarios and reserve build-up due to loan growth.
PB segment - The ACL amounted to $89 million, a decrease of $3 million quarter-over-quarter, reflecting lower reserves for
construction loans by $2 million, mostly due to lower loan volumes.
Provision for credit losses
Provision for loan losses of $59 million for the third quarter of 2026 decreased by $6 million when compared to the prior
quarter, primarily driven by lower reserve requirements in the PB segment and favorable changes in the macroeconomic
scenarios. Including the provision for unfunded loan commitments and the provision related to the Corporation’s investment
portfolio, the provision for credit losses for the third quarter was $59 million.

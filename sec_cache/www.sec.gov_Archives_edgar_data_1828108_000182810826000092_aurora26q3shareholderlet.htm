A letter to shareholders With Aurora-Driver powered trucks operating without a person behind the wheel, accelerating production, and expanding customer adoption, we have reached an inflection point on our path to commercial scale. We have 20+ driverless trucks already built and coming into the fleet, and production is accelerating. Demonstrating high utilization, in September the Aurora Driver surpassed 500,000 commercial driverless miles, maintaining 100% on-time delivery, since launch. The production momentum equips us to meet growing customer demand. Following our recent Analyst & Investor Day, we hosted a Partner Summit with leaders from over 35 of the largest and most innovative carriers and private fleets. This engagement supported another landmark Driver as a Service (DaaS) memorandum of understanding (MOU) with Charger Logistics. 23Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

 
Expanding commercial demand Building on an initial TaaS agreement signed earlier this year, Charger Logistics has selected Aurora to scale their autonomous fleet with an MOU to own and operate 500 trucks through our DaaS business model, which represents a potential multi-year revenue stream in the hundreds of millions of dollars. With truck delivery expected in 2027 and 2028, Charger plans to use the Aurora Driver to add capacity on its network and drive higher utilization, starting between Dallas and Laredo, one of its busiest freight routes in the Sun Belt. As customers experience the Aurora Driver operating reliably on the road and see the compelling economic benefits firsthand, we are building a strong foundation to expand into larger, long-term commitments. Since launching autonomous operations along the Laredo corridor, we’ve realized immediate operational benefits that point to a clear path to scaling. Aurora’s plans to open long-haul, high-volume routes position us to scale geographically while we integrate autonomy from our TMS to the road, building a resilient and autonomous ecosystem for our customers.” —ANDY KHERA, PRESIDENT, CHARGER LOGISTICS 33Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

 
Increasing operational versatility Our scaling plan prioritizes driverless delivery between customer sites to enhance the Aurora Driver’s value proposition. In November, the Aurora Driver will be operating directly to sites for several driverless customers, including automated navigation through security checkpoints. Over the coming months, we will begin site-to-site driverless operations for the majority of our customers. Beyond customer sites, a true end-to-end, nationwide service requires the Aurora Driver to operate within existing freight infrastructure just like conventional trucks. Historically, autonomous trucks have relied on voluntary enhanced pre-trip inspections (EPTI) to bypass weigh stations. With our weigh station traversal capability, the Aurora Driver can now autonomously determine if a station is open, navigate to an available scale lane, and adapt to different scale configurations. This capability makes it easier for customers to integrate the Aurora Driver into their networks.

 
Advancing regulatory alignment As the Aurora Driver’s capabilities continue to expand, the regulatory landscape increasingly supports scaled deployment. At the federal level, the U.S. Department of Transportation (USDOT) is advancing a unified framework to accelerate commercial deployment while asserting global technological leadership. Under the Automated Vehicles National Strategy (FY 2026–2030), USDOT established a clear roadmap built on the premise that autonomous driving technology offers a generational leap in roadway safety by reducing error factors such as distraction, fatigue, and impairment. The Trump Administration is committed to safely unleashing American innovation on our roads. We’re doing so because of the exciting promise new technologies like AVs have in reducing roadway fatalities, enhancing mobility, and fundamentally reshaping how people move for the better. This strategy outlined a plan to empower our innovators to build faster, smarter and safer than ever before.” —SEAN DUFFY, U.S. TRANSPORTATION SECRETARY The strategy expands upon the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration’s 2025 AV Framework and defines specific action items across four foundational pillars: • Prioritize Safety Provide guardrails to support and improve safety as the AV industry grows. • Provide Regulatory Certainty Modernize existing regulations, remove unnecessary barriers, streamline processes, and provide clarity and predictability. Work towards consistency. • Promote American Innovation Expand commercial availability and operation for passenger and freight applications domestically and promote U.S. interests abroad. • Facilitate Interoperable Corridors Convene national stakeholders to promote seamless cross-State interoperability within the transportation system. 53Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

 
A recent action by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) shows what this modernization looks like in practice. Earlier this month, FMCSA granted Aurora and the industry an important, five-year exemption, permitting driverless trucks to use cab-mounted warning beacons in place of traditional, manually-placed roadside warning devices when a commercial motor vehicle is stopped. By successfully demonstrating that our warning beacon technology would likely achieve a level of safety equivalent to or greater than legacy requirements, Aurora’s leadership in working with federal regulators has cleared the way for driverless freight at scale for the industry. In the exemption, FMCSA also recognized “that updating the underlying warning device requirements for all CMVs, not just ADS-equipped CMVs, may be ripe for future regulatory action.” This may be the first of many examples where safety technology being tested and deployed by autonomous vehicles will eventually be deployed on all commercial vehicles to make our roads safer, which Aurora fully supports. By eliminating the need for a driver to exit the cab physically, walk along an active highway or shoulder, and manually place warning devices, the beacons prevent drivers from being exposed to the risk of being struck by oncoming traffic.” —DEREK BARRS, FMCSA ADMINISTRATOR FMCSA Administrator Derek Barrs announcing warning device exemption at the Autonomous Vehicle Industry Association (AVIA) Autonomy Summit fireside chat with Aurora President, Ossa Fisher 63Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

 
While existing state law already provides a clear path for our planned multi- state expansion, USDOT’s regulatory modernization would complement these state pathways by fostering national consistency. This federal alignment would further strengthen long-term operational predictability, enhance broader public trust, and maintain U.S. leadership in autonomous technology. The bipartisan BUILD America 250 Act would replace the state-by- state maze with one clear set of strong, transparent federal safety rules, letting American companies build, test and improve their technology here rather than taking their investment elsewhere.” —GARRET GRAVES, FORMER MEMBER OF CONGRESS In addition, we are seeing a growing number of voices calling for the rapid passage of the BUILD America 250 Act, recognizing the importance of the autonomous trucking provisions that are included in the legislation today. 73Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

 
Accelerating truck production To meet growing customer demand for this critical technology, we are executing a phased manufacturing plan, beginning with upfitting at Roush and initial lineside integration with Volvo, while preparing for full OEM industrialization with Volvo and PACCAR. Together with Roush, we are working to establish an annualized production capability of more than 1,000 trucks. While our initial ramp volume over the past month was constrained by material availability, component quality, and issues identified during end-of-line testing, our team moved quickly to address these pre-production bottlenecks. With weekly output steadily improving, we now expect to achieve our targeted production rate of 20 trucks per week in early December. Importantly, this is a starting point - Roush has considerable ability to scale production higher through line expansion. The facility is fully operational, fully staffed, and all tooling and fixtures are validated for high-volume execution. We are actively building trucks 16 hours a day, providing Aurora with the dedicated manufacturing capacity needed to seamlessly ramp output alongside customer demand.” —BRAD RZETELNY, VP, CONTRACT MANUFACTURING, ADVANCED COMPOSITES, PROGRAM MANAGEMENT, ROUSH

 
Our strategic partnership with Volvo continues to advance, driven by deep operational alignment and growing customer demand. This is a deeply co-engineered partnership where our teams have been working side-by-side from design to commercialization. We have built a strong foundation together – merging our purpose built and purpose designed Volvo VNL Autonomous with the Aurora Driver. Now we jointly look forward to introducing fully driverless operations early next year, helping address capacity challenges in the transport industry while giving customers safe, reliable deliveries and consistent performance, day after day.” —NILS JAEGER, PRESIDENT, VOLVO AUTONOMOUS SOLUTIONS The dialogue with fleets has fundamentally shifted from testing technology to capturing efficiency. From the desk of our CFO Third quarter 2026 revenue totaled $3 million across driverless and vehicle operator-supervised commercial loads. The Aurora Driver achieved another record number of commercial miles driven during the quarter. Third quarter operating loss, including stock-based compensation (SBC), totaled $257 million. Excluding SBC of $54 million, R&D totaled $160 million, SG&A was $38 million, and cost of revenue was $8 million. We used approximately $170 million in operating cash during the third quarter of 2026 and capital expenditures totaled $36 million. This cash spend was within our externally communicated quarterly average target. We expect cash use of approximately $250 million during the fourth quarter of 2026 as we continue to scale our driverless fleet. During the third quarter, we issued 6 million shares of Class A common stock through our ATM program for net proceeds of $41 million. We used $23 million of these proceeds to fund the tax liability associated with the vesting of employee restricted stock units. In turn, we ended the quarter with a very strong balance sheet, including over $1 billion in cash & short-term investments. Reflecting a production ramp about six weeks behind our prior plan, we now expect 2026 revenue of approximately $9 million, which continues to represent substantial year-over-year growth. We anticipate exceeding the 200-truck milestone by the end of February. Building on this foundation and with a steady-state production rate of approximately 20 trucks per week expected by early December, we remain on track to exit 2027 with more than 1,000 driverless trucks in operation. David Maday CFO 123Q26 SHAREHOLDER LETTER

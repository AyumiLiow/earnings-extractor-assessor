MERCER INTERNATIONAL INC. REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS

Selected Highlights

 

•
As announced on November 2, 2026, we entered into restructuring support agreements with significant support from noteholders and shareholders to, among other things, effectuate a balance sheet restructuring which will address $1.275 billion of our outstanding senior notes and provide for $400 million of fully backstopped new capital to position Mercer for long-term success. Our operating subsidiaries will continue to operate their businesses with no impact on customers, suppliers and employees
•
Third quarter Operating EBITDA* improved to negative $2.9 million (net loss of $59.2 million), including a non-cash inventory impairment of $8.0 million, from negative $28.1 million (net loss of $80.8 million) in the same quarter of 2025
•
Secured a total of approximately $26.5 million in grants and repayable contributions for the Peace River mill to improve productivity and modernize the mill amid industry headwinds
•
Continued efforts to improve our balance sheet and optimize working capital. "One Goal One Hundred" program remains on track to achieve $100 million of cost savings and operational efficiencies by year end, with cost savings of $22.0 million in third quarter and a total of approximately $76.0 million to date since launch in April 2025

 

NEW YORK, NY, November 5, 2026 ‑ Mercer International Inc. (Nasdaq: MERC) today reported third quarter 2026 Operating EBITDA of negative $2.9 million, an improvement from negative $28.1 million in the same quarter of 2025 and negative $21.0 million in the second quarter of 2026.

 

In the third quarter of 2026, net loss was $59.2 million ($0.88 per share) compared to $80.8 million ($1.21 per share) in the third quarter of 2025 and $76.0 million ($1.13 per share) in the second quarter of 2026.

 

Mr. Juan Carlos Bueno, Chief Executive Officer, stated: "As announced on November 2, 2026, we entered into restructuring support agreements with holders of more than 87% of the aggregate outstanding principal amount of

our senior notes and with holders of a majority of the outstanding shares of our common stock that provide for a comprehensive recapitalization of the company (the "Recapitalization"). The transaction provides for, among other things, the exchange of approximately $1.1 billion of existing senior notes for equity, eliminates approximately $96 million of annual cash interest on our senior notes and provides for $400 million of new capital through a fully-backstopped rights offering of senior secured notes. The Recapitalization is expected to significantly reduce our debt, extend our maturity runway and provide substantial new liquidity. This transaction is a decisive step forward and a result of constructive negotiations with key stakeholder groups.

 

The Recapitalization is being implemented through Mercer International Inc., the parent company, and Mercer International (Texas) LLC, a non-operating subsidiary. No other subsidiary is directly impacted by the implementation of the plan of reorganization. Our operations will continue in the ordinary course with no impact on customers, suppliers and employees.

 

During the third quarter of 2026, our pulp sales realizations remained steady. Industry-wide curtailments and permanent pulp mill closures helped balance out supply this quarter, but soft market demand and ongoing regional overcapacity in China kept prices flat. Overall fiber pricing improved as we had lower costs for our pulp mills in Canada and improved supply for our German sawmills. In contrast, fiber costs for our German pulp mills increased due to persistent regional supply shortages and competition from energy producers. Our results in the third quarter of 2026 were also negatively impacted by significant planned annual maintenance downtime during the quarter.

 

Per unit fiber costs for the pulp and solid wood segments modestly decreased in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the second quarter. For the fourth quarter, per unit fiber costs for our German pulp mills are expected to increase slightly, driven by continued strong demand for wood as an energy source amid ongoing geopolitical conflicts, and reduced supply. Conversely, per unit fiber costs for our Canadian pulp mills are expected to decrease following renegotiation of Celgar's supply contracts and improved supply, while sawmill per unit fiber costs are projected to remain flat.

 

In the third quarter of 2026, our pulp mills had 44 days of planned annual maintenance downtime (approximately 48,900 ADMTs) which negatively impacted Operating EBITDA in the quarter by approximately $34.1 million. We currently expect a total of 18 days of planned annual maintenance downtime at the Celgar mill in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Following Rosenthal's planned downtime in the third quarter of 2026, the mill encountered effluent treatment issues during startup. To address these issues and as a precautionary measure, the mill proactively paused its restart process and wastewater discharge. After working closely with relevant authorities, wastewater discharge subsequently resumed and the mill restarted following 24 days of unplanned downtime in the fourth quarter of 2026.

 

In the third quarter of 2026, overall pulp prices were relatively flat compared to the second quarter. In the fourth quarter of 2026, we currently expect softwood pulp prices to modestly increase in all our markets due to continued global supply constraints. For hardwood pulp, prices are expected to remain relatively stable.

 

Our lumber sales realizations modestly increased in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the second quarter, primarily due to higher prices in the U.S. market driven by reduced supply. While supply also contracted in Europe, continued weak regional demand limited the positive impact on pricing in the market. In the fourth quarter of 2026, we currently expect lumber prices to be relatively flat as weak demand, driven by high interest rates and seasonal slowdowns, is offset by ongoing supply constraints. As market conditions improve, we believe pent-up demand and supply constraints will support price increases. The order book and commitments for our mass timber business are approximately $151 million at the end of the third quarter, anchored by large-scale data center infrastructure projects. We expect these contracts to begin contributing to our results as they commence in late 2026 and into 2027.

 

In the third quarter of 2026, we successfully secured government support for our Peace River mill in the form of approximately $13.0 million in grants and up to $13.5 million in low-interest repayable contributions. This funding directly supports our projects to improve productivity and modernize the mill during a period of macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty for the industry.

 

Our "One Goal One Hundred" program achieved an additional $22.0 million in cost savings in the third quarter and approximately $76.0 million in total since launch. We remain on track to achieve our previously disclosed $100 million target by the end of 2026. The program is part of broader initiatives we have implemented to improve our balance sheet and preserve cash, including efforts to reduce capital expenditures and optimize working capital."

 

Mr. Bueno concluded: "Market and industry-wide pressures continue to persist as we enter the fourth quarter of 2026. With the support of the majority of our noteholders and shareholders, we are putting in place a much stronger balance sheet. As we work to complete the Recapitalization, our focus remains on business fundamentals, where cost reduction, operational efficiency, and cash flow preservation are key. We will also continue exploring opportunities for

Consolidated – Three Months Ended September 30, 2026 Compared to Three Months Ended September 30, 2025

Total revenues for the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 4% to $440.7 million from $458.1 million in the same period of 2025 primarily as a result of lower pulp sales volumes and realizations. This decrease was partially offset by higher energy and solid wood products sales realizations.

Costs and expenses in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 9% to $479.4 million from $525.7 million in the same period of 2025. This decrease was primarily driven by lower pulp sales volumes and the benefits of our cost reduction initiatives, partially offset by higher per unit fiber and freight costs. In the third quarter of 2026, costs and expenses included a $13.0 million benefit from government grants recognized at our Peace River mill and a non-cash inventory impairment of $8.0 million primarily as a result of low pulp prices and high fiber costs. In the same period of 2025, costs and expenses included a non-cash inventory impairment of $20.4 million primarily as a result of lower pulp prices.

In the third quarter of 2026, Operating EBITDA improved to negative $2.9 million from negative $28.1 million in the same period of 2025. This improvement was primarily due to the government grants recognized at our Peace River mill, cost reduction initiatives and higher energy and solid wood products sales realizations. These positive impacts were partially offset by higher per unit fiber and freight costs and lower pulp sales realizations. In the third quarter of 2026, our Operating EBITDA included a non-cash inventory impairment of $8.0 million compared to $20.4 million in the same period of 2025.

In the third quarter of 2026, Segment Operating EBITDA for our pulp segment improved to positive $5.9 million from negative $12.7 million in the same period of 2025. This improvement was primarily due to government grants recognized at our Peace River mill, cost reduction initiatives and higher energy sales realizations. These positive impacts were partially offset by higher per unit fiber costs and lower pulp sales realizations. In the third quarter of 2026, Segment Operating EBITDA included a non-cash inventory impairment of $7.0 million compared to $18.9 million in the same period of 2025.

Pulp segment revenues, comprised of pulp, energy and chemical revenues, in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 8% to $310.2 million from $339.0 million in the same period of 2025 due to lower pulp revenues, partially offset by higher energy and chemicals revenues.

Pulp revenues in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 12% to $281.1 million from $318.6 million in the same period of 2025 as a result of lower sales volumes and realizations.

In the third quarter of 2026, the third-party industry quoted average list price in North America and net price in China for NBSK pulp both decreased compared to the same period of 2025. The decrease was primarily due to weak demand driven by the current economic environment and, in China, an oversupplied paper market. In the third quarter of 2026, the third-party industry quoted average list price for NBSK pulp in Europe increased compared to the same period of 2025 but the benefit of the higher list price was mostly offset by increased customer discounts. Our average NBSK pulp sales realizations in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 8% to $671 per ADMT from $728 per ADMT in the same period of 2025.

In the third quarter of 2026, the third-party industry quoted average list price in North America and net price in China for NBHK pulp both increased from the same period of 2025, primarily due to continued global supply constraints. In the third quarter of 2026, average NBHK pulp sales realizations increased by approximately 14% to $601 per ADMT from $528 per ADMT in the same period of 2025.

Total pulp sales volumes in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 7% to 421,413 ADMTs from 452,840 ADMTs in the same period of 2025 primarily driven by reduced production.

Energy and chemical revenues in the third quarter of 2026 increased by approximately 43% to $29.1 million from $20.4 million in the same period of 2025 primarily due to higher energy sales realizations.

Costs and expenses in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 14% to $327.3 million from $378.6 million in the same period of 2025. This decrease was primarily driven by lower pulp sales volumes and the benefits of our cost reduction initiatives, partially offset by higher per unit fiber costs at our German mills. In the third quarter of 2026, costs and expenses included a $13.0 million benefit from government grants recognized at our Peace River mill and a non-cash inventory impairment of $7.0 million. In the same period of 2025, costs and expenses included a non-cash inventory impairment of $18.9 million.

Total pulp production in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by approximately 11% to 406,519 ADMTs from 458,708 ADMTs in the same period of 2025. This decrease was mainly due to more days of planned annual maintenance downtime, reduced production at our German pulp mills in response to high fiber prices and mechanical issues at our Celgar mill. In the third quarter of 2026, our pulp mills had 44 days of planned annual maintenance downtime (approximately 48,900 ADMTs). In the same quarter of 2025, our pulp mills had 32 days of downtime (approximately 35,700 ADMTs) which included 20 days of planned annual maintenance and 12 days of unplanned downtime at the Celgar mill.

We estimate that planned annual maintenance downtime in the third quarter of 2026 adversely impacted our Segment Operating EBITDA by approximately $34.1 million, comprised of approximately $22.6 million in direct out-of-pocket expenses and the balance in reduced production. Many of our competitors that report their financial results using International Financial Reporting Standards capitalize their direct costs of maintenance downtime.

Overall average per unit fiber costs in the third quarter of 2026 increased by approximately 7% compared to the same period of 2025 primarily due to higher costs in Germany. These higher costs were driven by reduced supply, and strong demand for wood as an energy source amid ongoing geopolitical conflicts. For the fourth quarter of 2026, per unit fiber costs for our German pulp mills are expected to slightly increase due to continued strong demand. For our Canadian pulp mills, per unit fiber costs are expected to decrease following renegotiation of Celgar’s supply contracts and improved supply.

Consolidated – Nine Months Ended September 30, 2026 Compared to Nine Months Ended September 30, 2025

Total revenues for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 were relatively steady at $1,390.3 million compared to $1,418.6 million in the same period of 2025 as lower pulp sales realizations and volumes were mostly offset by higher energy and solid wood products sales realizations.

Costs and expenses in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 were relatively flat at $1,520.9 million compared to $1,537.8 million in the same period of 2025. The decrease in costs and expenses from lower planned annual maintenance downtime, cost reduction initiatives and lower pulp sales volumes was mostly offset by higher per unit fiber and freight costs and the negative foreign exchange impact from a weaker dollar. In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, costs and expenses included a $13.0 million benefit from government grants recognized at our Peace River mill and inventory impairment charges of $59.0 million primarily as a result of low pulp prices and high fiber costs. In the same period of 2025, costs and expenses included inventory impairment charges of $31.4 million against pulp inventory.

In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, Operating EBITDA decreased to negative $16.0 million from negative $1.9 million in the same period of 2025. This decrease was primarily due to higher per unit fiber and freight costs and lower pulp sales realizations. These adverse effects were partially offset by the impact of lower planned annual maintenance downtime, cost reduction initiatives, higher energy and solid wood products sales realizations and the government grants recognized at our Peace River mill. In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, our Operating EBITDA included inventory impairment charges of $59.0 million compared to $31.4 million in the same period of 2025.

Liquidity

On November 2, 2026, we entered into restructuring support agreements (the “RSAs”) with holders of more than 87% of the aggregate outstanding principal amount of our 12.875% Senior Notes due 2028 and 5.125% Senior Notes due 2029, including the members of an ad hoc group of noteholders, and with holders of a majority of the

outstanding shares of our common stock. The RSAs set out the terms of a comprehensive Recapitalization that is expected to significantly reduce our debt, extend our maturity runway and provide substantial new liquidity.

To implement the transactions, Mercer International Inc. and Mercer International (Texas) LLC, a non-operating U.S. subsidiary, expect to commence voluntary chapter 11 prepackaged cases under the United States Bankruptcy Code (the “Chapter 11 Cases”) and file a plan of reorganization (the “Plan”) in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division (the “Court”). Completion of the Recapitalization is subject to customary conditions, including receipt of any requisite regulatory approvals, and we are optimistic that we will be able to conclude this expedited chapter 11 process by the end of 2026.

For further information regarding the Recapitalization, please refer to our Current Report on Form 8-K dated November 2, 2026.

After taking into account the previously announced €70 million reduction in borrowing capacity under our German revolving credit facility, we had approximately $72.5 million available under our revolving credit facilities, bringing aggregate liquidity to approximately $143.5 million as of September 30, 2026. Please refer to our Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q for the period ended September 30, 2026, for further information regarding our liquidity as of September 30, 2026.

MERCER INTERNATIONAL INC.

COMPUTATION OF OPERATING EBITDA

(Unaudited)

(In thousands)

 

Operating EBITDA is defined as operating income (loss) plus depreciation and amortization and long-lived asset impairment charges. Management uses Operating EBITDA as a benchmark measurement of its own operating results, and as a benchmark relative to its competitors. Management considers it to be a meaningful supplement to operating income (loss) as a performance measure primarily because depreciation expense and long-lived asset impairment charges are not actual cash costs, and depreciation expense varies widely from company to company in a manner that management considers largely independent of the underlying cost efficiency of our operating facilities. In addition, management believes Operating EBITDA is commonly used by securities analysts, investors and other interested parties to evaluate our financial performance.

 

Operating EBITDA does not reflect the impact of a number of items that affect our net income (loss), including financing costs, income taxes and the effect of derivative instruments. Operating EBITDA is not a measure of financial performance under GAAP and should not be considered as an alternative to net income (loss) or operating income (loss) as a measure of performance, nor as an alternative to net cash from (used in) operating activities as a measure of liquidity. Operating EBITDA is an internal measure and therefore may not be comparable to other companies.

 

Operating EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure at the consolidated level and is considered different from Operating EBITDA at the segment level, referred to as "Segment Operating EBITDA", which is our single measure of segment profit or loss presented in our financial statements under GAAP. For more information on Segment Operating EBITDA, refer to the segment information note within our consolidated financial statements.

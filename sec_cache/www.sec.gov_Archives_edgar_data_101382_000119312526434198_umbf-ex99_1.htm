KANSAS CITY, Mo. (October 27, 2026) – UMB Financial Corporation (Nasdaq: UMBF), a financial services company, announced net income available to common shareholders for the third quarter of 2026 of $272.3 million, or $3.56 per diluted share, compared to $271.8 million, or $3.56 per diluted share, in the second quarter of 2026 (linked quarter) and $180.4 million, or $2.36 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025.

 

 

 

Net operating income available to common shareholders, a non-GAAP financial measure reconciled later in this release to net income available to common shareholders, the nearest comparable GAAP measure, was $272.4 million, or $3.56 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $273.0 million, or $3.57 per diluted share, for the linked quarter and $206.5 million, or $2.70 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2025. Operating pre-tax, pre-provision income (operating PTPP), a non-GAAP measure reconciled later in this release to the components of net income before taxes, the nearest comparable GAAP measure, was $373.0 million, or $4.88 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $380.1 million, or $4.97 per diluted share, for the linked quarter, and $293.4 million, or $3.84 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2025. These operating PTPP results represent a 1.8% decrease on a linked-quarter basis and a 27.2% increase compared to the third quarter of 2025.

 

“Our third-quarter results demonstrate the strong momentum across UMB’s diversified lines of business, reflected in robust balance sheet and fee income growth, strong asset quality metrics, and a stable net interest margin that together produced superior risk-adjusted returns,” said Mariner Kemper, UMB Financial Corporation chairman and chief executive officer.

 

“Gross loan production reached a record $3.1 billion during the quarter, while net loan growth, after payoffs and paydowns, totaled $1.4 billion. Average commercial and industrial loan balances increased approximately 22% on a linked-quarter annualized basis, driving an 11% annualized increase in total average loans compared with the prior quarter. Our asset quality also improved, as of September 30, 2026, nonperforming loans declined approximately $10 million to $118 million, or 28 basis points of total loans, compared to 31 basis points as of June 30, 2026.”

 

Kemper continued, “While persistent inflation, the conflict in the Middle East, and higher short- and long-term interest rates may create headwinds, customer sentiment and demand for business investment across our markets and business lines remain strong, as demonstrated by our record third quarter production. We believe UMB’s interest-rate-neutral balance sheet and diversified financial services model position us well to navigate the anticipated environment. This includes a continued focus on innovation and evolution, most recently demonstrated in our Fund Services division with the continued expansion of its exchange-traded fund (ETF) servicing business, which now offers a fully in-house operating model that provides asset managers with flexible ETF servicing solutions and seamless support.”

 

“We remain focused on growing both sides of the balance sheet, maintaining a consistent net interest spread, preserving strong asset quality, and continued momentum in our fee businesses.”

•

This information supplements the results that are reported according to generally accepted accounting principles in the United States (GAAP) and should not be viewed in isolation from, or as a substitute for, GAAP results. The differences between the non-GAAP financial measures – net operating income available to common shareholders, operating EPS, operating ROE, operating ROA, operating noninterest expense, operating efficiency ratio, operating PTPP, operating PTPP EPS, operating PTPP-FTE, operating PTPP-FTE EPS, tangible common shareholders’ equity, and tangible book value per share – and the nearest comparable GAAP financial measures are reconciled later in this release. The company believes that these non-GAAP financial

 

 

measures and the reconciliations may be useful to investors because they adjust for acquisition- and severance-related items, and the FDIC special assessment that management does not believe reflect the company’s fundamental operating performance.

 

Net operating income available to common shareholders for the relevant period is defined as GAAP net income available to common shareholders, adjusted to reflect the impact of excluding expenses related to Day 1 acquisition provision expense, acquisitions, severance expense, the FDIC special assessment, and the cumulative tax impact of these adjustments.

 

Operating EPS (diluted) is calculated as earnings per share as reported, adjusted to reflect, on a per share basis, the impact of excluding the non-GAAP adjustments described above for the relevant period. Operating ROE is calculated as net operating income available to common shareholders, divided by the company’s average total common shareholders’ equity for the relevant period. Operating ROA is calculated as net operating income available to common shareholders, divided by the company’s average assets for the relevant period. Operating noninterest expense for the relevant period is defined as GAAP noninterest expense, adjusted to reflect the pre-tax impact of non-GAAP adjustments described above. Operating efficiency ratio is calculated as the company’s operating noninterest expense, net of amortization of other intangibles, divided by the company’s total non-GAAP revenue (calculated as net interest income plus noninterest income, less gains on sales of securities available for sale, net).

 

Operating PTPP income for the relevant period is defined as GAAP net interest income plus GAAP noninterest income, less noninterest expense, adjusted to reflect the impact of excluding expenses related to acquisitions and severance, and the FDIC special assessment.

 

Operating PTPP-FTE for the relevant period is defined as GAAP net interest income on a fully tax equivalent basis plus GAAP noninterest income, less noninterest expense, adjusted to reflect the impact of excluding expenses related to acquisitions and severance, and the FDIC special assessment.

 

Tangible common shareholders’ equity for the relevant period is defined as GAAP common shareholders’ equity, net of intangible assets. Tangible book value per share is defined as tangible common shareholders’ equity divided by the company’s total common shares outstanding.
